The real problem

Leadership development has a follow-through problem.

You know what good leadership looks like. You could teach the frameworks. And yet under pressure, the same reactions show up and the same conversations go sideways.

The drive, high standards and need to prove yourself that got you here are often running on old fuel. When they stop working, the instinct is to reach for more of the same: another book, another system, more discipline.

The missing piece isn't more information. It's the inner work that makes any of it possible.

  • You stay calm in the meeting and replay it at 2 a.m.
  • You know you should delegate and still end up redoing the work.
  • You avoid the hard conversation until it becomes a crisis.
  • You know something has to change and still find yourself pushing harder.
  • You've succeeded by every external measure and still feel like it's never enough.

The approach

Self-awareness as the central discipline of leadership.

Not self-optimization or performance hacks. The harder, slower work of actually knowing yourself, which is where lasting change in how you lead comes from.

About

Richard Reynolds, LMFT

For eight years I worked as a clinician, and I kept hearing the same story from clients: the impact their leaders and bosses were having on their lives. It made one thing clear to me. How someone leads ripples out far beyond the office.

Leadership has always been part of my own path. I've never been part of a company or organization where I didn't end up in a leadership role. But I noticed that almost no one was positioned to help leaders do the inner work that real change requires.

That's what I do now. I bring a clinician's depth to leadership coaching, so we work with what's actually driving the patterns, not just the surface behavior.
